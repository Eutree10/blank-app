## 2. Training philosophy for this plan

1. **Polarized / 80-20.** Roughly 80% of weekly running **easy** (truly conversational), 20% **hard**
   and focused. You're *already* doing this well (easy runs at 130–155 bpm, 2 quality days) — the job
   is to keep protecting it as training gets harder, not to overhaul it.
2. **Threshold first, VO₂max second, race-pace last.** Classic, reliable sequencing — build the
   aerobic engine and lactate threshold before sharpening top-end speed and race pace. (No glaring
   weakness to chase; this is just the proven order.)
3. **Consistency > heroics.** Six steady weeks beat one big week and a layoff. At 15, *durability*
   is the goal as much as fitness.
4. **Age-appropriate volume.** We build mileage gradually (≤~10%/week, with down weeks). You're still
   growing — bone, tendon, and growth-plate loading must lag behind cardiovascular ambition.

## 3. Weekly schedule (template)

Your two hard group days are **Tuesday and Thursday**. Two quality days is the right number — the only
catch is they're close together (just Wednesday between them), so we **differentiate them**: make one
*threshold* and one *VO₂max/speed* rather than two max-effort hammerings, and keep everything else
genuinely easy.

| Day | Session | Purpose | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Easy + 6×20s strides | Recovery, neuromuscular | ≤160 bpm · 4:40–5:10/km |
| **Tue** | **Group — KEY 1: THRESHOLD** (tempo / cruise intervals) | Aerobic ceiling — your weak spot | 175–180 bpm |
| **Wed** | Easy (full recovery before Thu) | Aerobic base | ≤160 bpm |
| **Thu** | **Group — KEY 2: VO₂max / speed** (the group's fast reps) | Top-end, economy | 181–190 bpm |
| **Fri** | Rest or easy 20–30 min | Absorb the Tue+Thu block | very easy |
| **Sat** | Easy + strides (group social run, kept easy) | Base | ≤160 bpm |
| **Sun** | **Long run** | Aerobic strength (underpins a fast 5k) | ≤165 bpm · 4:40–5:00/km, build 12→16 km |

> **Why threshold on Tue, speed on Thu:** Tuesday lands on the freshest legs of your two-day block, so
> give it the session that matters most for *your* gap — threshold. Thursday's group speed reps then
> sharpen on slightly tired legs, which is fine for VO₂max. If your group's fixed plan is the reverse,
> just swap — the point is **one threshold + one VO₂max, not two of the same.**

**The discipline that buys you sub-17:** on Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat, **keep HR ≤160 bpm.** With a group it's
tempting to race the easy runs too — don't. Easy genuinely easy is what makes the two hard days work.

**Volume:** you're at **45–55 km/week — that's a good, sufficient base; do not rush it up.** The win is
shifting the *intensity mix*, not adding kilometres. Hold 45–55, and only drift toward ~55–60 in
autumn if everything feels easy. **Down week every 4th week** (cut ~25%).

## 4. The 7-month macrocycle (Jun → Dec 2026)

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Base** | Jun → mid-Jul | Easy volume, strides, **strength/mobility habit**, light hills | Builds the aerobic base your 10k says is missing |
| **2 — Threshold** | mid-Jul → Aug | Tempo runs, cruise intervals at ~3:45–3:52/km | Raises lactate threshold — the core of the 61s gain |
| **3 — VO₂max + 5k specific** | Sep → Oct | 3–5 min intervals at 3:15–3:25/km | Lifts the ceiling, teaches goal pace under fatigue |
| **4 — Sharpen + tune-ups** | Nov | Race-pace reps, 1–2 tune-up races (parkrun/3k) | Race execution, pacing, confidence |
| **5 — Taper + attempt** | early–mid Dec | Cut volume ~40–50%, keep intensity sharp | Peak freshness for the **sub-17 attempt** |

Target a **flat, fast, paced 5k or parkrun in early–mid December** (cool weather helps). Build in a
**backup race ~2 weeks later** so one bad-weather day doesn't end the year.

## 6. Strength, mobility & durability (non-negotiable at 15)

Your data shows **no injuries — protect that asset.** 2× per week, 15–20 min, bodyweight-focused:
- **Posterior chain & feet:** calf raises, single-leg hops, hamstring bridges → tendon/bone resilience for a growing skeleton.
- **Core & hips:** planks, side planks, glute bridges, clamshells → holds form when fatigued (the back half of your 5k).
- **Plyometric drills:** A-skips, bounding (light) → running economy, which directly lowers your time at the same effort.
- **Mobility:** 5 min daily ankle/hip routine.

No heavy barbell maxing needed at your age — bodyweight, drills, and consistency win.

## 7. Nutrition (15-year-old endurance athlete)

You're 65 kg / 170 cm — a **healthy, appropriate racing weight. Do not try to lose weight to run
faster.** At 15 you're still developing; under-fueling causes **RED-S** (low energy availability),
which tanks performance, stalls growth, and weakens bone. Speed comes from training, not the scale.

- **Carbohydrate is the priority fuel** — base every meal around it (rice, pasta, oats, potatoes, bread, fruit). Eat a carb-rich meal/snack within ~60 min after key sessions.
- **Protein:** ~1.4–1.6 g/kg/day (~90–105 g) spread across meals for recovery and growth.
- **Iron — your single biggest watch-item.** Teen endurance runners are the highest-risk group for low
  ferritin, which mimics "bad fitness" (heavy legs, fading late — exactly your 10k pattern). Eat iron-rich
  foods (red meat, beans, fortified cereals, spinach) **with vitamin C**; ask a doctor for a **ferritin
  blood test** before you ramp up. (See red flags.)
- **Calcium + vitamin D** for bone (dairy/fortified alternatives) — critical while growing and loading.
- **Hydration:** habitual through the day, not just around runs.
- **Fuel the school walk + 6 runs:** don't train hard on an empty tank, especially morning sessions.

## 8. Recovery & monitoring

- **Sleep 8.5h is excellent — guard it fiercely.** It's where 15-year-olds adapt and grow. Don't trade it for anything.
- Track **morning resting HR** and (if your watch supports it) **HRV** — a sustained jump in resting HR
  or drop in HRV = back off. This is the early-warning system your race data *can't* show.
- The **"talk test"** on easy days is your most important discipline. If you can't speak full sentences, you're going too hard.
- Watch for: lingering fatigue, dropping enthusiasm, getting slower at the same effort, frequent
  illness → all signs to insert an extra easy/rest day. **More rest, not more miles, fixes these.**

## 10. What to actually fix

There's **no glaring weakness in your valid data** — easy days are easy, volume is sound, intensity
count is right. So sub-17 is about **steady, well-sequenced progression**, not fixing something broken:

1. **Make one of your two hard days a true threshold session** (Tue, 175–180 bpm). Groups tend to favour
   short fast reps, which feed the speed your 2.1-mile PB already shows. Threshold is the proven core
   workout for 5k strength and the one *emphasis* your data leans toward — but it's a refinement, not a rescue.
2. **Easy days — already nailed (confirmed 130–155 bpm).** Well inside the easy zone; the common
   "grey-zone leak" isn't happening for you. Keep doing exactly this as training intensifies.
3. **Build a consistent Sunday long run** (12 → 16 km, easy, ≤165 bpm). Aerobic strength underpins a
   fast 5k and is the most common thing a speed-leaning runner is light on.

### Other cautions
1. **Re-test paces as you improve** — the §5 paces are from your current 18:00 fitness; recalibrate after every race.
2. **Ignore the humidity 10k.** It told us nothing about fitness. If you want a true endurance read,
   race or time-trial a 10k (or a hard tempo) in cool conditions — *that's* a usable data point.
3. **A baseline ferritin test is still worth it.** Not because of that 10k, but because teen endurance
   runners are the highest-risk group for low iron, which silently flattens performance. Cheap insurance.
4. **Resting HR 53 is a baseline to watch.** Track it each morning: a sustained rise of ~7+ bpm =
   under-recovery → back off. It should *drift down* as your aerobic base deepens.
5. **You're 15 and still growing.** Cardiovascular ambition outruns skeletal readiness. Holding volume
   at 45–55 km, down weeks, strength work, and your 8.5h sleep are how you reach sub-17 *without* a stress fracture.
6. **Don't chase weight.** 65 kg is fine. Restricting to "get lighter" (RED-S) is the fastest way to ruin a promising season. Fuel the work.
