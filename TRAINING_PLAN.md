## 2. Training philosophy for this plan

1. **Polarized / 80-20.** Roughly 80% of weekly running **easy** (truly conversational), 20% **hard**
   and focused. Your data screams that you've likely been running too many runs in the "comfortably
   hard grey zone" — that builds the speed you already have but neglects the aerobic base you're
   missing. Easy days must be *easy*.
2. **Threshold first, VO₂max second, race-pace last.** Sequence the build to attack your specific
   weakness (endurance) before sharpening the strength you already own (speed).
3. **Consistency > heroics.** Six steady weeks beat one big week and a layoff. At 15, *durability*
   is the goal as much as fitness.
4. **Age-appropriate volume.** We build mileage gradually (≤~10%/week, with down weeks). You're still
   growing — bone, tendon, and growth-plate loading must lag behind cardiovascular ambition.

## 4. The 7-month macrocycle (Jun → Dec 2026)

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Base** | Jun → mid-Jul | Easy volume, strides, **strength/mobility habit**, light hills | Builds the aerobic base your 10k says is missing |
| **2 — Threshold** | mid-Jul → Aug | Tempo runs, cruise intervals at ~3:45–3:52/km | Raises lactate threshold — the core of the 61s gain |
| **3 — VO₂max + 5k specific** | Sep → Oct | 3–5 min intervals at 3:15–3:25/km | Lifts the ceiling, teaches goal pace under fatigue |
| **4 — Sharpen + tune-ups** | Nov | Race-pace reps, 1–2 tune-up races (parkrun/3k) | Race execution, pacing, confidence |
| **5 — Taper + attempt** | early–mid Dec | Cut volume ~40–50%, keep intensity sharp | Peak freshness for the **sub-17 attempt** |

Target a **flat, fast, paced 5k or parkrun in early–mid December** (cool weather helps). Build in a
**backup race ~2 weeks later** so one bad-weather day doesn't end the year.

## 6. Strength, mobility & durability (non-negotiable at 15)

Your data shows **no injuries — protect that asset.** 2× per week, 15–20 min, bodyweight-focused:
- **Posterior chain & feet:** calf raises, single-leg hops, hamstring bridges → tendon/bone resilience for a growing skeleton.
- **Core & hips:** planks, side planks, glute bridges, clamshells → holds form when fatigued (the back half of your 5k).
- **Plyometric drills:** A-skips, bounding (light) → running economy, which directly lowers your time at the same effort.
- **Mobility:** 5 min daily ankle/hip routine.

No heavy barbell maxing needed at your age — bodyweight, drills, and consistency win.

## 7. Nutrition (15-year-old endurance athlete)

You're 65 kg / 170 cm — a **healthy, appropriate racing weight. Do not try to lose weight to run
faster.** At 15 you're still developing; under-fueling causes **RED-S** (low energy availability),
which tanks performance, stalls growth, and weakens bone. Speed comes from training, not the scale.

- **Carbohydrate is the priority fuel** — base every meal around it (rice, pasta, oats, potatoes, bread, fruit). Eat a carb-rich meal/snack within ~60 min after key sessions.
- **Protein:** ~1.4–1.6 g/kg/day (~90–105 g) spread across meals for recovery and growth.
- **Iron — your single biggest watch-item.** Teen endurance runners are the highest-risk group for low
  ferritin, which mimics "bad fitness" (heavy legs, fading late — exactly your 10k pattern). Eat iron-rich
  foods (red meat, beans, fortified cereals, spinach) **with vitamin C**; ask a doctor for a **ferritin
  blood test** before you ramp up. (See red flags.)
- **Calcium + vitamin D** for bone (dairy/fortified alternatives) — critical while growing and loading.
- **Hydration:** habitual through the day, not just around runs.
- **Fuel the school walk + 6 runs:** don't train hard on an empty tank, especially morning sessions.

## 8. Recovery & monitoring

- **Sleep 8.5h is excellent — guard it fiercely.** It's where 15-year-olds adapt and grow. Don't trade it for anything.
- Track **morning resting HR** and (if your watch supports it) **HRV** — a sustained jump in resting HR
  or drop in HRV = back off. This is the early-warning system your race data *can't* show.
- The **"talk test"** on easy days is your most important discipline. If you can't speak full sentences, you're going too hard.
- Watch for: lingering fatigue, dropping enthusiasm, getting slower at the same effort, frequent
  illness → all signs to insert an extra easy/rest day. **More rest, not more miles, fixes these.**

   to do more quality — resist it. The gain is in the easy aerobic miles you're probably under-doing.
